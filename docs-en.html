How it works

Messages come in. They turn into knowledge. You reply better.

You don't need to be technical. The system does the heavy lifting behind the scenes and hands you everything ready to use, in three simple moves.

01

Everything arrives together

Every message from every channel lands in the same inbox. WhatsApp, email, Instagram and the rest sit side by side, without you switching apps.

02

It gets organized

The assistant reads every message, turns it into tidy text, tags the topic and gathers everything from the same customer into one contact. It's kept in a map of relationships it checks in milliseconds to reply with context.

03

You reply better

With the full history on hand and a suggested reply ready, you answer on the same channel or switch channels with one click. Always with your approval.

The channels

Six ways to talk. One single contact.

The same person messages you on WhatsApp today and by email tomorrow. The system recognizes it's them and keeps everything together, in one history.

WhatsApp main

Voice

Calls with a natural voice that answers, talks and records what was said. Perfect for confirming, reminding or giving a quick reply.

Instagram

Direct messages and comments from your profile become conversations here. You can even set a rule that sends an automatic DM to anyone who comments an agreed word on your post.

Telegram

Telegram chats come into the same inbox. Handy for anyone with customers or communities that prefer this app.

SMS

Plain old text messages. They come and go through the same panel, great for alerts that need to arrive even without internet.

Email

Set up integrations

Turning on a channel is always the same recipe.

You don't write a single line of code. Open the integrations screen, pick the channel, paste the keys that service hands you and flip the switch. Done, the channel's screen shows up in the menu.

1

Open Integrations

In the panel's side menu, click Integrations.

2

Pick the channel

Choose WhatsApp, Voice, Instagram, Telegram, SMS or Email.

3

Paste the keys

Copy the details from the service and paste them into the fields shown.

4

Flip the switch

Turn it on and the channel's screen appears in the menu on its own. Simple as that.

What works the magic: the keys go into an encrypted vault on your machine and never sit in plain text. When you turn the channel on, the system recognizes the key and reveals that channel's screen on its own, no reload. Register the key, the screen appears.

Step by step, channel by channel

WhatsApp easy

The most direct way to start. You connect a WhatsApp number by reading a code on the screen, just like when you open WhatsApp on your computer.

  1. Have a separate number handyWe recommend a SIM just for the business, not your personal one. Keep the phone nearby.
  2. Go to Integrations and pick WhatsAppClick connect. A square code (QR) appears on the screen.
  3. Scan the code with your phoneIn WhatsApp on your phone: Linked devices, Link a device, and point the camera at the screen.
  4. Done, you're connectedConversations start showing up and the history downloads on its own.
The code on screen refreshes on its own every half minute. If it changes before you scan it, no worries: that's normal and on purpose, for security. Just scan whatever is showing at the moment.
Voice (calls) medium

Calls bring together two pieces. The voice comes from ElevenLabs, which turns text into natural speech. The phone line comes from Twilio, which actually dials. You build a voice agent in ElevenLabs, set what it says, and paste both keys here.

  1. Create an ElevenLabs accountGo to elevenlabs.io and sign up. There's a free plan to try it out. Keep the API key, a long, secret string.
  2. Build a voice agentIn ElevenLabs, create an agent, pick the voice and write two instructions: the opening one, which it says when the call starts, and the conversation one, which guides how it replies.
  3. Connect TwilioTwilio gives you the number that places the call. Grab its credentials. If you already turned on SMS, they're the same ones.
  4. Paste it here and turn it onIn Integrations, pick Voice, paste the ElevenLabs key, the agent and the Twilio credentials, and activate.
Each call becomes a transcribed conversation inside the system, just like a text message. If the person prefers, you can carry on by WhatsApp from where the call left off.
Instagram (DMs and comments) advanced

Instagram belongs to Meta (the same owner as Facebook and WhatsApp). For the system to read your DMs and comments, Meta requires a few steps and an approval. It's worth it, but be honest about the timing: it can take a few days.

  1. Have a professional profileYour Instagram needs to be a business or creator account, linked to a Facebook page. You can change that in Instagram's own settings.
  2. Create an app on MetaAt developers.facebook.com, create an app. It's free. It's just the official bridge between Instagram and the system.
  3. Request the messaging permissionsIn the Meta panel, enable the Instagram messaging permissions and submit them for review. Meta reviews and approves.
  4. Copy the access tokenOnce approved, Meta gives you a token (a long key). Copy it and paste it into Integrations, choosing Instagram.
An honest prerequisite: Meta's approval is mandatory and out of our hands. Until it comes through, the channel is ready but on hold. As soon as it's approved, you just turn it on.
Once it's on, you can set a comment-to-DM rule: anyone who comments an agreed word on your post automatically gets a direct message. Remember that Instagram only lets you reply to a DM within 24 hours of the person's last message.
Telegram easy

On Telegram you talk to an assistant built into the app, called BotFather, and it hands you the key in seconds. It's the fastest channel to turn on after WhatsApp.

  1. Open BotFather in TelegramIn the Telegram app, search for BotFather (with the blue verified badge) and start the chat.
  2. Ask for a new botSend the start message and then create a new bot. It asks for a name and a username. Pick them and confirm.
  3. Get the tokenBotFather replies with a token (a string of numbers and letters). That's your access. Copy all of it.
  4. Paste it here and turn it onIn Integrations, pick Telegram, paste the token and activate. The Telegram screen appears in the menu.
Want the bot to read group messages? Ask BotFather for that in the group privacy settings. Other than that, it already works in direct chats.
SMS medium

SMS uses Twilio, the same company behind voice calls. If you already turned on the Voice channel, you reuse the Twilio credentials and just pick a sender number. If not, you create the account, grab a number and three details, and paste them here.

  1. Create a Twilio accountGo to twilio.com and sign up. There's trial credit to experiment before you pay.
  2. Get a numberIn the panel, rent a phone number. That's what your SMS will go out from and come in on.
  3. Copy the three detailsOn Twilio's home page you'll find the account identifier (Account SID), the secret key (Auth Token) and the number you rented. Copy all three.
  4. Paste them here and turn it onIn Integrations, pick SMS, paste the three details and activate.
Every SMS you send has a small cost charged by Twilio. Start with the trial credit to get a feel for the volume before you add a card.
Automatic opt-out: anyone who replies STOP stops getting your messages right away, as good practice requires. The system respects and logs that on its own.
Email (Gmail) advanced

For email the system connects with Gmail, from Google. Since it involves access to your inbox, Google asks for some setup and, depending on usage, a review. Nothing complicated, but it needs care at each step.

  1. Go into the Google consoleAt console.cloud.google.com, create a project. It's free and only serves to authorize the access.
  2. Enable access to GmailLook for the Gmail API in the list of services and enable it. That officially unlocks reading and sending emails.
  3. Generate the credentialsCreate a credential of the service-account or OAuth type. Google generates a file or a key pair. Keep it safe.
  4. Authorize and paste it hereOn the first connection, Google shows a screen asking for your permission. Accept it, then paste the credentials into Integrations, choosing Email.
An honest prerequisite: Google may ask for a project review if you're going to use it with many addresses. For personal use or a small team, it usually clears right away.
Each email exchange becomes a threaded conversation, tied to the right contact. You choose between simple mode, which checks the inbox every so often, and instant mode, which receives the moment the email arrives.
Once you turn on any channel, it shows up in the panel's side menu instantly, and conversations start arriving in the single inbox. You can turn one channel on today and another next month, at your own pace.
Day to day

Nine tools, all in plain human language.

Each screen in the panel has a clear purpose. Here's what each one does and how to use it, no fluff.

Hub

Conversations

The single inbox. Every channel side by side, each conversation carrying the colored icon of the channel it came from.

Filter by channel with one click and reply from inside the conversation itself.
Find

Search

Searches by exact word and also by meaning, mixing the two. Type roughly what you remember and it finds it, even in different words. It runs 100% on your machine and works even without internet.

Write the topic ("that quote from June") and let the search do the rest.
People

Contacts

The full list of who talks to you, in a rich table you shape: show or hide columns and filter by what matters at the moment.

Click a contact to see everything about them gathered, from every channel.
Relationships

Graph CRM

A visual map showing how people, companies and topics connect. See who referred whom and who talks to whom.

Drag the dots and click to open each one's card.
Flow

Funnel

A board of columns driven by the agent: it reads the conversation's intent and places each contact in the right column on its own. An optional feature, turn it on only if you want.

Define what moves a contact to each column and what happens when they land there.
Results

Conversion

A dashboard with clear numbers: how many came in, how many became customers and where people stop, with colorful charts broken down by channel.

Look at the charts to understand what's working and what got stuck.
Reach-outs

Follow-ups

Reminders to check back in with someone. The system writes the draft, waits for your yes to send and then keeps an eye on whether the person replied.

Review what it suggested and approve, edit or discard it.
Overview

Summaries

Automatic summaries of what happened, so you don't have to read everything. Great for a busy group: hours of chat turn into a few lines, written in a voice of their own that signs off at the bottom.

Read the daily summary and open only what actually needs you.
Collective

Groups

Intelligence about your WhatsApp groups, with a menu of actions per group: choose the agent's mode, the visibility, generate or schedule a summary, see stats and organize with tags.

Choose which groups to follow and tune out the noise from the rest.

Frequently asked questions

Questions everyone asks.

Do I need to know how to code to use it?
No. You only work in the panel, clicking and pasting. The keys the services ask for are text you copy from a site and paste here. No code, no terminal.
Do I have to turn on every channel at once?
Not at all. Start with WhatsApp, which is the quickest, and turn on the others whenever you like. Each channel is independent and shows up in the menu as soon as you activate it.
Why do Instagram and email take longer?
Because Meta (Instagram) and Google (email) require an official authorization before they grant access. That protects you and your contacts. The step by step is in the setup section, and the wait is on those companies, not on the system.
Does the assistant reply on its own without me seeing?
It drafts the reply, but sending depends on your approval. You stay in control of everything that goes out in your name.
Does the same person messaging on different channels become separate contacts?
No. The system recognizes it's the same person and merges everything into one contact. You see the full history, whether it came from WhatsApp, email or Instagram.
Does it cost money to use the channels?
The panel is yours. Some services behind it have their own cost: Twilio charges per SMS and per call, ElevenLabs charges for the voices depending on the plan. WhatsApp, Telegram, Instagram and email have free paths to get started. There's always a way to try before you spend.
Does my data go to the cloud?
Not to work. The system runs on your computer and the history stays with you. Each channel's services (WhatsApp, Twilio, Google) only see what passes through them, just as happens today when you use each one. The organizing, the search and the cards are local.
Does it work on my phone?
The panel was designed for the computer, which is where the system runs. You open it in the browser to keep up and set things up comfortably. It keeps working even with the tab closed, as long as the computer is on.
Is there a risk of WhatsApp blocking my number?
The system lowers that risk with a quiet-hours lock and a sending pace close to a human's, both always on. Even so, use a number dedicated to the business, not your personal one, and start slowly.
